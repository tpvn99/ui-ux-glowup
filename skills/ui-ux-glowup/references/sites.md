# Reference Library

The sites below are widely recognized for the quality of their design. For each: why it's a reference and **the mechanics to borrow**. Sites evolve — check them live when you have web access.

## SaaS, tools, tech products

**Linear** (linear.app)
- Tight sans-serif, large headings with negative tracking, heavily muted secondary text
- Short left-aligned hero followed by a large product shot
- Real product screenshots, often in slight perspective or cropped, fading into the background
- Sections separated by space, not colored backgrounds; micro-labels above headings
- Restrained, fast animations, never decorative

**Stripe** (stripe.com)
- Visible 4-column grid with thin vertical lines, content aligned to it
- Visual explanations: mini-diagrams, code snippets, animated fake UIs instead of icons
- Very clear hierarchy: heading → paragraph → "Learn more →" link
- High information density that stays readable; many sections that vary in shape

**Vercel** (vercel.com)
- Hairline borders (1px, low contrast) everywhere, "bento" card grids sharing their borders
- Geist typeface, monospace for technical details
- Subtle grid or dot background, extreme restraint, lots of black/white

**Raycast** (raycast.com)
- Product UI staged like a real app (window chrome, visible keyboard shortcuts `⌘K`)
- Feature cards with visuals specific to each feature, no generic icons

**Resend / Clerk / Cal.com** (resend.com, clerk.com, cal.com)
- Dev-first landings: real code snippets, per-language tabs, short sections
- Product components embedded directly in the page (the real widget, not an image)

**Attio** (attio.com) and **Notion** (notion.com)
- App screenshots with credible real-looking data, zooms on UI details
- Notion: in-house line illustrations, warm tone, readable large type

## Fintech, premium B2B

**Mercury** (mercury.com) and **Ramp** (ramp.com)
- Serious and high-end: lots of air, editorial headings, polished photography
- Numbers and proof up front (customers, amounts), sober customer-logo strip
- Feature cards with rebuilt mini-UIs (transactions, charts)

## E-commerce, consumer brands

**Apple** (apple.com)
- One idea per screen, short strong heading, huge product, neutral background
- Alternating rhythm: full-bleed hero → tile grid → detailed technical section
- Typography as a graphic element; sticky product sub-navigation

**Aesop, Allbirds, Glossier** (aesop.com, allbirds.com, glossier.com)
- Product pages: large gallery on the left, sticky buy column on the right, details in accordions
- Consistent photography (same light, same framing), lots of white

**Airbnb** (airbnb.com)
- Cards led by the image, rounded corners, minimal text below
- Search as the central element, filters as scrolling pills
- Excellent mobile handling and states (skeletons, empty states)

## Agencies, portfolios, studios

**Award-winning sites on Awwwards / Godly** (awwwards.com, godly.website)
- XXL editorial typography, asymmetric grids, work shown large
- Refined transitions and hovers; cursor tricks and effects reserved for a few key moments
- Tone down for conservative clients: keep the structure, reduce the effects

**Pentagram** (pentagram.com)
- Dense project grid, strong images, minimal text and impeccable typography

## Local business, trades, services, health

No single "star": borrow the reassuring mechanics of premium brands.
- Split hero with text / real photo of the work (Aesop, Mercury for restraint)
- Concrete proof next to the CTA: Google rating, years in business, service area
- Services as a structured list or photo cards, not generic icons
- Numbered process steps (Stripe-style "how it works")
- Persistent contact CTA on mobile (bottom bar)

## Dashboards, apps, back-offices

**Linear (app)**, **Vercel (dashboard)**, **Stripe Dashboard**, **shadcn/ui** (ui.shadcn.com)
- Compact sidebar, 16px icons + 13–14px labels, collapsible groups
- Thin top bar with breadcrumb + `⌘K` search
- Dense tables, 40–48px rows, tabular-nums, actions on hover
- Settings pages in 2 columns: title + description on the left, fields on the right
- Sober KPI cards with sparklines, no colored backgrounds
